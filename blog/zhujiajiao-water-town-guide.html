Zhujiajiao Water Town Guide: How to Visit the "Venice of Shanghai"

Why Visit Zhujiajiao

Often called the "Venice of Shanghai", Zhujiajiao (朱家角) is a 1,700-year-old water town just 50 km from downtown Shanghai. Weathered stone bridges, mirror-still canals, and whitewashed houses with upturned eaves make it a perfect half-day escape from the skyscrapers.

How to Get There from Shanghai

  • Metro Line 17 to Zhujiajiao station, then a short bus or walk — about 70–90 minutes
  • Tour bus from People's Square to Zhujiajiao bus station
  • Private tour — the easiest and most comfortable: door-to-door with a guide and driver (recommended for combining with Yu Garden or the Bund)

Top Sights in Zhujiajiao

Fangsheng Bridge (放生桥)

The largest stone arch bridge in the Shanghai area, built during the Ming-Qing transition. Its five arches span the Caohe River and offer the classic postcard view of the town.

North Street (北大街)

The narrow main shopping lane, packed with food stalls selling zongzi (rice dumplings), braised pork knuckles, and local rice wine.

Kezhi Garden (课植园)

A grand private garden built by a wealthy merchant — part classical garden, part Western-style manor, perfect for a quiet stroll.

The Canals & Bridges

Beyond the main streets, boat rides along the canals thread beneath dozens of small bridges — the essence of water-town life.

🍽️ Must-Try Local Foods

Zhujiajiao specialties: Zhashui zongzi (pork-stuffed rice dumplings wrapped in bamboo leaves), braised pork knuckle, "green-shelled" beans, and a cup of local rice wine by the water.

Best Time to Visit

Visit on a weekday morning to enjoy the town before the crowds arrive; weekends are noticeably busier. Spring and autumn offer the most pleasant weather for canal-side walks.
