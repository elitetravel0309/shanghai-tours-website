Shanghai Transportation Guide 2026: Metro, Taxi, Maglev & How to Get Around

Getting Around Shanghai

Shanghai has one of the most comprehensive public transportation systems in the world. With a modern metro network, reliable taxis, the world's fastest commercial train, and easy ride-hailing apps, getting around the city is efficient and affordable.

Shanghai Metro (地铁)

The Shanghai Metro is the backbone of city transport and the most convenient way to reach most attractions. It's fast, clean, affordable, and operates in both Chinese and English.

  • Lines: 11+ lines covering 278+ stations across the city
  • Hours: Approximately 5:30 AM – 11:00 PM daily
  • Fares: ¥3–9 depending on distance (about $0.40–$1.25 USD)
  • Payment: Shanghai Metro card (交通卡), WeChat Pay, Alipay, or contactless bank cards at ticket machines
  • English signage: Full English announcements and station names on all lines

💡 Metro Pro Tip

The metro is the fastest and cheapest way around Shanghai during peak hours when roads are congested. Line 2 connects Pudong Airport to the city center, and Line 10 gives you easy access to the Bund, Yu Garden, and Lujiazui.

Taxis (出租车)

Shanghai's taxis are plentiful and relatively affordable. Look for official taxis from reputable companies:

  • Starting fare: ¥10.8 for the first 5 km (about $1.50 USD)
  • Per km rate: ¥1.6 per additional km
  • Recommended companies: 大众 (Dazhong), 强生 (Qiangsheng), 锦江 (Jinjiang)
  • Payment: Cash, WeChat Pay, Alipay
  • Tip: Always have your destination written in Chinese characters — many drivers don't speak English

Where to find taxis: Taxi stands at hotels, shopping malls, metro stations, and major attractions. You can also hail them on the street (look for the green "空车/vacant" light on the roof).

Maglev Train (磁悬浮)

The Shanghai Maglev is the world's fastest commercial train and a must-try experience for transport enthusiasts:

  • Route: Longyang Road Station ↔ Pudong International Airport
  • Journey time: Just 7 minutes!
  • Top speed: 431 km/h (268 mph)
  • Fare: ¥50 single / ¥80 return (about $7/$11 USD)
  • Frequency: Every 15–20 minutes

🚀 Maglev Experience

The Maglev is more of a sightseeing experience than pure transportation. Sit on the right side for the best views as you rocket past the city skyline. Even if you're not catching a flight, it's worth the trip just for the thrill.

Public Bus (公交车)

Shanghai's bus network is extensive but can be confusing for first-time visitors. Best used when your destination isn't near a metro station.

  • Regular bus: ¥1 per ride
  • Air-conditioned bus: ¥2 per ride
  • Payment: Exact change (coins), Shanghai Metro card, or mobile payment
  • Tip: Buses have Chinese-only signage — use a map app to track your stop

Ride-Hailing (网约车)

China's ride-hailing apps are convenient and widely used:

  • DiDi (滴滴) — China's Uber equivalent, available in English
  • Amap/Gaode (高德) — Popular map app with integrated ride-hailing
  • Payment: WeChat Pay or Alipay (international credit cards may have limited support)
  • Tip: DiDi's English interface makes it the best choice for tourists

Sightseeing Bus (观光巴士)

Several hop-on hop-off bus services operate in Shanghai, covering major attractions along the Bund, Nanjing Road, and the French Concession. These are great for first-time visitors who want an overview of the city with commentary in multiple languages.

Essential Transportation Tips

  • Get a Shanghai Metro Card (交通卡) — works on metro, buses, and some taxis; saves time at turnstiles
  • Travel off-peak when possible — metro and roads are packed during 7:30–9:30 AM and 5:30–7:30 PM
  • Metro is king — it's the most reliable and fastest way to get around; avoid road traffic entirely
  • Download offline maps — Google Maps may be unreliable; use Amap (高德) or Baidu Maps
  • Airport transfers: Metro Line 2 or Maglev to Pudong Airport; Metro Line 10 to Hongqiao Airport
  • Book a private car for airport transfers if you have heavy luggage or arrive late at night
