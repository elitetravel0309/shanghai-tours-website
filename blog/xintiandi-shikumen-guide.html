Xintiandi & Shikumen: Shanghai's Restored Old Laneways

What Is Xintiandi?

Xintiandi ("New Heaven and Earth") is a stylish pedestrian district in central Shanghai built around traditional shikumen houses — the stone-gate courtyard homes that once housed most of the city's middle class. Restored rather than rebuilt, the lanes now mix century-old architecture with cafés, restaurants, boutiques and galleries.

What Are Shikumen Houses?

Shikumen ("stone-gate") homes are a distinctly Shanghai invention from the late 19th century — a hybrid of Western terraced housing and traditional Chinese courtyard design, framed by a grand stone gate at the entrance. For much of the 20th century they were the city's most common dwelling. Walking them now gives a real sense of old Shanghai's street life.

What to See & Do

  • Stroll the north & south blocks — one more upscale, one more boutique, both photogenic.
  • Admire the shikumen architecture — stone gates, brickwork and courtyard layouts.
  • People-watch at open-air cafés and dine at restaurants tucked into the lanes.
  • Shop for crafts and souvenirs among the boutiques.
  • Visit the former site of the First National Congress of the CCP nearby for a dose of history.

Practical Information

  • Getting there: metro Line 1 or Line 10 to South Huangpi Road (Xintiandi) station.
  • Pace: 1-2 hours of relaxed wandering covers it.
  • Best time: late afternoon into evening, when the lanes and cafés light up.
