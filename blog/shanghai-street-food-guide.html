Shanghai Street Food: What to Eat, Where to Find It

Shanghai's Street-Food Scene

Shanghai is one of China's great food cities, and much of its best eating happens on the street — from steamer baskets to sizzling griddles. Here's your cheat sheet to the dishes you shouldn't miss and where to track them down.

The Essential Dishes

Xiaolongbao (Soup Dumplings)

These delicate steamed dumplings hold a spoonful of hot broth inside. Bite the top, sip the soup, then eat the rest. Nanxiang style at Yu Garden is the classic; you'll find thousands of iterations city-wide.

Shengjianbao (Pan-Fried Pork Buns)

A crispy-bottomed, soup-filled bun, grilled to order — richer and more rustic than xiaolongbao, and a Shanghai favourite.

Scallion Oil Noodles

Simple, fragrant wheat noodles tossed with scallion oil and soy — comfort food in a bowl, often served with a braised egg.

Rice & Snack Specialities

Look for sugar-pork mooncakes, stinky tofu, and sweet red-bean pastries at night-markets and old-town lanes.

Where to Find It

  • Yu Garden / City God Temple area: Shanghai's best-known snack cluster, great for soup dumplings.
  • Nanjing Road Food Street: tourist-friendly stalls in the centre.
  • Night markets: look for local favourites like Songhu dialect grill and dessert stalls in the evening.
  • Side lanes off the French Concession: hole-in-the-wall spots with locals queuing — a good sign.

How to Eat Like a Local

Share dishes, eat with the flow of the market, and don't fear the queue — long lines usually mean fresh food. A guide takes the guesswork out, helping you order and explaining each dish. A Few Quick Tips

  • Carry small change for market stalls.
  • Watch how locals eat new dishes — there's usually a knack (like the soup-dumpling bite).
  • Most vendors accept Alipay/WeChat Pay.
  • Trust your nose and the crowd.
