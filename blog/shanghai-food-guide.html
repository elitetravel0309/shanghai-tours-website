Shanghai Food Guide 2026: Must-Try Dishes & Best Restaurants

Shanghai's Culinary Heritage

Shanghai's cuisine — known as Haipai (海派) cooking — is a sophisticated fusion that blends flavors from northern and southern China with Western influences. The local style emphasizes sweet, savory, and soy-braised flavors, fresh seasonal ingredients, and meticulous presentation. From humble street stalls to Michelin-starred restaurants, Shanghai is a food lover's paradise.

Must-Try Shanghai Dishes

小笼包 — Xiaolongbao (Soup Dumplings)

The crown jewel of Shanghai cuisine. These delicate steamed dumplings are filled with seasoned pork and a burst of rich, savory soup. The secret is the thin, almost translucent skin that holds the liquid inside. Best enjoyed with black vinegar and shredded ginger on the side.

Where to try: 南翔馒头店 (Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant) at Yu Garden is the most famous — expect a queue. For a less crowded experience, try Din Tai Fung (鼎泰丰) or Jia Jia Tang Bao.

生煎包 — Shengjianbao (Pan-Fried Buns)

Shanghai's beloved breakfast staple. These pan-fried buns are cooked in a large flat pan until the bottoms turn golden and crispy, while the tops remain soft and fluffy. Inside, juicy pork filling and hot soup await. The crispy-bottom-soft-top texture contrast is what makes shengjianbao irresistible.

Where to try: 小杨生煎 (Yang's Fried Dumplings) has multiple locations and consistently excellent quality.

蟹壳黄 — Xiekehuang (Crab Shell Pastry)

Named for its resemblance to a crab shell, this flaky, sesame-topped pastry comes in both savory (with pork and scallion) and sweet (with red bean paste) versions. The exterior is golden and crunchy, while the inside is layered and buttery.

Where to try: Street stalls near Nanjing Road and the Old City (Nanshi) area.

排骨年糕 — Paigu Niangao (Pork Chops with Rice Cakes)

A classic Shanghai comfort food: crispy fried pork chops served alongside chewy, sweet rice cakes in a savory brown sauce. The combination of textures — crunchy meat, sticky rice cakes, and rich sauce — is deeply satisfying.

Where to try: 曙光饭店 (Shuguang Hotel Restaurant) and 鲜得来 (Xian De Lai) are the iconic spots.

馄饨 — Wonton

Shanghai has two styles of wonton: 大馄饨 (large wontons) filled with shepherd's purse and pork, served in clear broth, and 小馄饨 (small wontons) with pure pork filling in a light, silky soup. Both are comforting and delicious year-round.

更多本地美食

  • 红烧肉 (Hong Shao Rou) — Shanghai-style braised pork belly, caramelized in soy sauce and sugar
  • 葱油拌面 (Cong You Ban Mian) — Scallion oil noodles, simple but incredibly aromatic
  • 腌笃鲜 (Yan Du Xian) — Spring bamboo shoot and pork soup, a seasonal delicacy
  • 大闸蟹 (Da Zha Xie) — Hairy crab, best in autumn (September–November)
  • 油爆虾 (You Bao Xia) — Quick-fried river shrimp in sweet soy glaze

Best Food Streets & Markets

云南南路 (Yunnan South Road)

Shanghai's most famous food street, packed with local eateries serving everything from xiaolongbao to grilled skewers. It's a one-stop destination for sampling a wide range of Shanghai snacks in a lively atmosphere.

黄河路 (Huanghe Road)

Known for its vibrant nightlife dining scene and local restaurants. Famous for its 大闸蟹 (hairy crab) restaurants in autumn and late-night supper spots.

昌里路夜市 (Changli Road Night Market)

A bustling night market that comes alive after dark with street food stalls, grilled meats, stinky tofu, bubble tea, and local snacks. Perfect for adventurous eaters who want an authentic local experience.

Top Restaurant Recommendations

  • 鼎泰丰 (Din Tai Fung) — World-famous for xiaolongbao; multiple locations in Shanghai
  • 南翔馒头店 (Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant) — The birthplace of Shanghai xiaolongbao at Yu Garden
  • 老正兴 (Lao Zheng Xing) — Historic restaurant serving traditional Shanghai cuisine since 1862
  • 绿波廊 (Lv Bo Lang) — Upscale Shanghainese dining at Yu Garden, once hosted Queen Elizabeth II
