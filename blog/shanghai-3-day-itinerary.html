Shanghai 3-Day Itinerary: The Perfect First-Timers Plan

Why a 3-Day Shanghai Trip Is Perfect

Three days is the ideal length for a first trip to Shanghai. You get enough time to soak up the modern skyline of Pudong, wander the old-town lanes, and still escape to a classic water town without feeling rushed. This guide lays out a balanced plan you can follow almost exactly, with an option to hand the logistics to a private guide.

Day 1: The Bund, Old Town & the Skyline

Start where Shanghai's story is most visible. Day 1 focuses on the city core.

Morning — The Bund

Walk the Bund (Waitan) promenade along the Huangpu River, with the historical banks on one side and the Pudong skyscrapers on the other. It's photo-perfect at any hour; early morning is calmest. Head down Nanjing Road, China's most famous shopping street, to feel the city's energy.

Afternoon — Yu Garden & Old City

Dip into Yu Garden and the surrounding Old City bazaar. The classical Ming-dynasty garden is small but exquisite — rockeries, koi ponds, and zigzag bridges. Note it's closed on Mondays. Nearby, the City God Temple area buzzes with snacks and tea houses.

Evening — Shanghai Tower & the Lights

Take the high-speed lift up the Shanghai Tower (632 m) for the best sunset view over the city. After dark, the Bund's skyline lights up — a classic end to day one. If you'd rather let a local handle the route, our Shanghai Classic Full-Day Tour covers the Bund, Yu Garden and Tower in one seamless day.

Day 2: Concessions, Culture & Food

Morning — French Concession

Explore the leafy lanes of the Former French Concession — plane trees, art-deco apartments, and boutique cafés along Wukang Road and Tianzifang. It's Shanghai's most atmospheric quarter and a photographer's dream.

Afternoon — Museums or Jade

Choose culture at the Shanghai Museum (bronzes, ceramics, calligraphy) or heritage at the Jade Buddha Temple. Both reward a slow visit. See them together on the Yu Garden & Jade Buddha Temple tour.

Evening — Street Food

Sample Shanghai's xiaolongbao (soup dumplings), scallion oil noodles and sugar-pork mooncakes. A guided Shanghai Street Food & Night Market tour is the tastiest and safest way to dive in.

Day 3: A Water-Town Escape

Use Day 3 to leave the skyscrapers behind. Zhujiajiao (the "Venice of Shanghai") is the most convenient canal town, about an hour from the city. Stroll under stone bridges, ride a canal boat, and try zongzi rice dumplings. Want to stay closer? A Suzhou day trip — classical gardens and silk — is equally rewarding. Plan the route yourself or join the 3-Day Shanghai & Water Town tour.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: the metro is cheap, fast and English-signed; a private car is easier for tourists.
  • Cash & payments: Alipay/WeChat Pay are everywhere; carry some CNY as a backup.
  • Visa: check whether you qualify for Shanghai's 240-hour visa-free transit.
  • Hotels: stay in or near central districts for easy access (ask us for recommendations).
