Tour Overview

Shanghai is a food lover's paradise, and this tour takes you straight to the heart of its culinary scene. Follow your foodie guide through hidden alleyways, bustling food streets, and lively night markets as you taste the dishes that define Shanghai's unique food culture.

From the iconic soup dumplings (xiaolongbao) to sizzling shengjian, hand-pulled noodles, and sweet desserts — this tour is a feast for all senses. Along the way, you'll learn about the history and traditions behind each dish and discover where locals really eat.

Shanghai is a food lover's paradise, where East meets West in a symphony of flavors. Known as "the Paris of the East," the city's culinary scene is a delicious blend of traditional Jiangnan cuisine and international influences. From sizzling street-side stalls to hidden noodle shops tucked away in historic lane houses (弄堂 lòngtáng), every corner of Shanghai offers a new taste adventure.

Your local foodie guide will take you beyond the tourist traps to the places where Shanghainese locals have been eating for generations. You'll start on Jiashan Road, a popular food street where the aroma of shengjianbao (pan-fried pork buns) fills the air. Then explore the legendary Yunnan Road Food Street, known for its BBQ skewers, crayfish, and stinky tofu. Along the way, you'll learn about the cultural significance of each dish and the stories behind the family-run stalls that have been serving them for decades.

Tour Highlights

🥟

Xiaolongbao (Soup Dumplings)

Learn the art of eating these delicate soup-filled dumplings without burning your mouth! Watch as expert chefs fold each dumpling by hand at a famous local house.

🥟

Shengjian (Pan-Fried Buns)

Try these legendary crispy-bottomed pork buns from a 50-year-old family stall. The perfect contrast of crunchy bottom and soft, juicy filling.

🍜

Shanghai-Style Noodles

Watch a master noodle-puller in action and taste the freshest scallion oil noodles (葱油拌面) you'll ever have — a Shanghai breakfast staple.

🌙

Yunnan Road Night Market

Dive into one of Shanghai's most famous food streets. Sample BBQ skewers, crayfish, stinky tofu, and more from dozens of bustling stalls.

🍡

Sweet Treats & Desserts

End on a sweet note with sugar-coated hawthorn skewers (糖葫芦), warm tangyuan (glutinous rice balls), and traditional eight-treasure rice pudding.

🍵

Tea Pairing Experience

Enjoy a traditional Chinese tea pairing to cleanse your palate between tastings, learning about the different tea varieties from your guide.

🦐

Shanghai Braised Pork (Hongshao Rou)

Taste the iconic red-braised pork belly — tender, sweet, and savory, it's the quintessential Shanghai comfort food.

🥘

Hidden Food Alley Exploration

Navigate through secret food alleys near the Old City, tasting scallion pancakes, tofu pudding, and other local favorites from hidden stalls.

Full Itinerary

5:00 PM

Hotel Pickup & Jiashan Road Food Street

Your foodie guide picks you up and takes you to Jiashan Road (嘉善路), a popular local food street. Start with classic Shanghai street foods: soup dumplings (xiaolongbao), pan-fried buns (shengjianbao), and scallion oil noodles. The tree-lined street is perfect for food photos!

6:00 PM

Shanghai Braised Pork & Local Specialties

Visit a beloved local eatery to taste Shanghai braised pork (hongshaorou), the city's signature dish — slow-cooked pork belly with a perfect balance of sweet and savory flavors. Also sample smoked fish and other traditional cold dishes.

7:00 PM

Hidden Food Alley Walk

Navigate through hidden food alleys near the Old City. Taste shengjian (pan-fried pork buns), scallion pancakes, and tofu pudding from family-run stalls that have been serving locals for decades. Your guide shares stories behind each dish.

7:45 PM

Yunnan Road Night Market

Dive into one of Shanghai's most famous food streets. The air fills with the aroma of BBQ skewers, sizzling crayfish, and the infamous stinky tofu (if you dare!). Sample 5-6 different stalls and taste the diversity of Chinese street food culture.

8:30 PM

Dessert & Traditional Tea

Finish with traditional sweet soup, sesame balls (majian), and sugar-coated hawthorn skewers. Your guide takes you to a local tea house for a soothing cup of Chinese tea and a brief tea-tasting session.

9:00 PM

Hotel Drop-off

Full and happy, your guide escorts you back to your hotel with a personalized list of restaurant recommendations for your remaining days in Shanghai.
